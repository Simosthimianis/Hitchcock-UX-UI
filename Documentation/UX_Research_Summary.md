# UX Research Summary

## Research Goal

The UX phase examined how users interact with Hitchcock-related websites and what they expect from a digital experience focused on the director's life and work.

The research preceded the final UI design and was intended to inform navigation, information architecture, content depth and visual communication.

## Competitive Analysis

The analysis examined three different kinds of Hitchcock-related experiences:

- an official promotional / brand-oriented site,
- a fan archive with stronger information depth,
- a research-heavy archive / database experience.

### Main Pattern

The research identified a clear trade-off:

- visually strong, mainstream experiences were easy to use but relatively shallow;
- research-oriented archives contained more material but often had dated interfaces, weak hierarchy or higher complexity.

The design opportunity was therefore to combine **cinematic presentation, usability and richer editorial content**.

## User Study

A small qualitative study was conducted with three participants representing different levels of:

- film knowledge,
- technological familiarity,
- experience browsing digital platforms.

### Tasks

Participants were asked to:

1. find information about Hitchcock's life,
2. locate films,
3. understand the purpose of the website,
4. browse the content,
5. freely explore according to their interests.

## Findings

### Positive

- Navigation was generally easy to understand.
- The visual style created a strong professional first impression.
- The interface was approachable even for participants with lower technology familiarity.
- Cinematic visual language was appreciated.

### Pain Points

- Content depth was insufficient for users interested in film analysis or research.
- Information on individual films was limited.
- Participants did not spend much time exploring multiple sections.
- The site's purpose could feel ambiguous between informational and promotional.

## Design Implications

These findings informed the final concept in several ways:

- stronger emphasis on filmography and deeper editorial content,
- clearer section-based navigation,
- dedicated Gallery and Director's Cut sections,
- use of recognizable visual hierarchy instead of database-style density,
- maintaining an atmospheric identity without sacrificing content structure.

## Research Scope

This was a small academic qualitative exercise rather than a statistically representative usability study. The findings were used as directional design input.
